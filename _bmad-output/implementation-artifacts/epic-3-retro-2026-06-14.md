## 2. What went genuinely well

- **The platform-pattern stack compounded across all five stories.** Story 3.1 established it: *pure-logic decision owner (EditMode-tested) + an OS-touching seam + a thin graceful-degrade `MonoBehaviour` view + live Bootstrap registration + tests in the existing `AR.Tests`*. 3.2 reused it (minus the seam — the safety gate needs no OS round-trip), 3.3 extended it with an async lifecycle, 3.4 applied it to two new seams at once, 3.5 absorbed and extended a seam. By 3.5 the *deviations* (3.2's no-seam gate, 3.5's seam-absorption-not-creation) were conscious, documented calls — not drift. This is the Epic-1 "atomic-mutation pattern compounded" lesson, recurring one tier up.

- **The seam/adapter split cleanly reconciled two architecture mandates that look contradictory.** Architecture says "AR contains no branching logic worth testing — thin adapter" AND "`ArSessionService` is the sole owner of the AR lifecycle." Every AR story resolved this the same way: the *decisions* worth testing (the state machine, the coaching gate, the spawn cap, the nearest-in-frustum selection) live in a pure-logic service tested headless against a fake; the *glue* the mandate targets (ARCore session, plane subsystem, occlusion shader, anchor re-acquire) lives in a `#if UNITY_ANDROID` adapter, stubbed-and-deferred. "There is no `ARSession`/plane subsystem in EditMode" is precisely *why* the seam exists — and it let the whole epic ship CI-green with zero device dependency.

- **Code review caught real correctness bugs in 3 of 5 stories — and they were a coherent class.** The standouts:
  - **3.3 (real bug):** `FirstLaunchGrant`-style — `ArSessionService`'s in-flight prewarm latch had a double-start hole on a `TearDown`-during-prewarm race (a second `PrewarmAsync` could fire a second `StartAsync`). Fixed by nulling the latch inside `TearDown`.
  - **3.4 (major):** the `Failed` placement path didn't clear a standing coaching banner — the success path reconciled the banner, the failure path forgot. (This birthed the [[failure-path-cleanup-parity]] memory.)
  - **3.5 (major):** `SelectRelocationTarget` had no NaN/Infinity guard — a degenerate distance seeded first would become a sticky winner (NaN comparisons are always false) → a NaN relocation pose. Fixed by skipping non-finite candidates.

- **Inherited deferrals were settled on schedule by their named owners.** This is the deferred-work ledger paying off exactly as designed:
  - The **1.2** `AnchorToken.IsValid`/`None` deferral → settled by **3.5** (its named owner).
  - The **3.1** camera-permission-re-check deferral → settled by **3.3** (its named owner).
  - The **3.4** seam-absorption mandate (a deferral 3.4 *created for itself to hand to 3.5*) → settled by **3.5**.
  - The **3.3** `OnArSessionInterrupted` consumer deferral → *partially* settled by 3.5 (the restore decision), remainder re-pointed to Epic 4. The implicit rule from Epic 1 held one tier up: *the story that owns the seam owns settling the deferral against it.*

- **GUID-preserving rename worked cleanly.** 3.5 renamed three files (`IArPlaneAnchorProvider`→`IArAnchorProvider`, `ArcorePlaneAnchorProvider`→`ArcoreAnchorProvider`, `FakeArPlaneAnchorProvider`→`FakeArAnchorProvider`) via `git mv` — the `.meta` GUIDs stayed intact (committed as 100% renames), so no Unity reference broke and the 3.4 forward-path tests stayed green through the rename. The VS phase's pre-flight grep confirmed the consumer-update list was complete before a single edit.

## 3. Recurring patterns — the real lessons

### Lesson 1 — The success path sets a contract the failure path must honour (NEW, recurring)
3.4's CR major and the general shape of 3.5's relocation logic are the same class: **when a method's success path performs a reconcile (clear a banner, raise an event, reset a flag), every other terminal path must mirror it or deliberately document why not.** The Blind Hunter layer reliably finds these. The fix is cheap (one line) but the discovery costs a patch + re-review cycle. Now captured as the [[failure-path-cleanup-parity]] memory and folded into the 3.5 story's Dev Notes as a pre-dev self-review item. **Takeaway:** during dev, scan every `return`/early-exit for side-effect parity before the CR phase; add a test that drives the *failure* path and asserts the reconcile fired.

### Lesson 2 — Float decisions need finiteness guards the moment they leave pure logic for device data (NEW)
3.5's NaN bug was latent: the pure-logic `SelectRelocationTarget` is correct against the fake (which supplies valid floats), but the field it ranks (`DistanceFromCamera`) is populated by the deferred device adapter (Story 6.3) — exactly where a degenerate camera-to-plane projection could produce NaN/Infinity. A NaN seed defeats every `<` comparison and becomes unbeatable. **Takeaway:** any pure-logic ranking/selection over a value that a *future device adapter* will populate must guard non-finite inputs now (skip them, never let one win), even though today's fake never produces them. The CR correctly placed this guard in the pure-logic decision, not deferred to the adapter — because the *selection* is the decision the story owns.

### Lesson 3 — "Verify, don't rebuild" is a real scope-saver when prior epics over-delivered the seam
3.5's AC-1 ("a serializable `AnchorToken` storable in `EncounterSnapshot`/`SaveModel`") was *already satisfied* — `EncounterSnapshotData.Anchors : AnchorToken[]` + `SaveJson` + `SaveRoundTripTests` were built back in Story 1.3's persistence work. The CS phase caught this by reading the actual codebase before writing the story, scoping AC-1 to "verify the round-trip + add the `IsValid` coercion" rather than re-implementing storage. **Takeaway:** before scoping a story's ACs, grep for what already exists — a lower epic's "reserve the seam without over-building" discipline (Epic 1's Lesson) means later stories often only need to *plug in*, not *build*.

### Lesson 4 — A "reinvention guard" left on a seam is load-bearing across stories
3.4 deliberately built its forward seam as `IArPlaneAnchorProvider` and left an XML-doc note on `TryCreateAnchor` instructing 3.5 to *absorb* it into the architecture-named `IArAnchorProvider` rather than add a parallel create-anchor surface. 3.5 acted on exactly that. Without the note, a 3.5 dev reading `architecture.md:409`'s prose name (`SaveAnchor`) could easily have added a third method. The VS phase flagged the residual ambiguity ("is `TryCreateAnchor` the save half, or do we add `SaveAnchor`?") and the story pinned it explicitly. **Takeaway:** when a story knowingly builds a partial/interim seam, write the absorption/extension contract *on the seam itself* (XML-doc), not just in the deferral ledger — the next dev reads the code first.

### Lesson 5 — The graceful-degrade view is the right altitude for the whole epic
Every AR story shipped its `MonoBehaviour` view as logic-free, `GameServices.Get`-guarded (catches `ServicesNotReadyException`/`KeyNotFoundException` → inert + `GameLog.Warn`, never throws at `Awake`), and *unplaced* (in no scene). This is the [[ui-presenter-pattern]] discipline holding across five stories: the pixels, the scene placement, and the routing are all honestly deferred to Epic 6, while the decision logic is 100% headless-proven now. No story tried to half-build a scene to "see it work" — the fake + the test IS the proof. (Consistent with Epic 1's "seams reserved without over-building.")
